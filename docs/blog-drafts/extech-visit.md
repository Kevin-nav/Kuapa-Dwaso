# Learning Through Partnership: Our Visit to Extech Agricultural Services

On Friday, 7 August 2026, Kuapa Dwaso representatives Ms Catherine Bruce Vochere and Mr Kevin Amisom Nchorbuno visited Extech Agricultural Services in Takoradi.

The purpose of the visit was to learn from people already working within Ghana’s agricultural sector and better understand the conditions Kuapa Dwaso must account for as we prepare for our first pilot.

The Extech team also took us to a warehouse, giving us an opportunity to see part of the physical infrastructure behind agricultural storage and trade. More importantly, our discussions helped us look beyond the building itself and think carefully about the relationships, demand, logistics and experience required to operate sustainably.

## Beginning with the market, not the building

One of the strongest lessons from the visit was that Kuapa Dwaso should not begin by constructing or operating its own warehouse.

Warehouses can play an important role in agricultural trade. They can provide storage, support aggregation and give farmers a buffer when immediate market conditions are unfavourable. However, they also require substantial investment and introduce continuing responsibilities involving maintenance, electricity, staffing, security and capacity utilisation.

A warehouse does not create demand by itself. If produce enters storage without reliable buyers, the business still carries the risk of low utilisation, spoilage and unsold stock.

This helped us refine our starting point. Kuapa Dwaso will initially focus on connecting dependable buyer demand with verified farmer supply and coordinating the movement of produce between them. We intend to begin through existing relationships, facilities and logistics partners rather than taking on the cost of physical infrastructure too early.

Owning warehouse infrastructure remains part of our longer-term ambition, currently projected at approximately three years. That decision will ultimately depend on evidence such as repeated transaction volume, storage demand, suitable locations, utilisation and the financial ability to operate a facility sustainably.

## Demand must be understood before produce moves

The discussion reinforced the importance of a demand-led approach.

Before produce is collected, Kuapa Dwaso needs to understand what a buyer requires:

- the crop and variety;
- the required quantity;
- the quality specification;
- the expected delivery location and date;
- the agreed price;
- and the buyer’s payment period.

Once those requirements are clear, we can identify farmers capable of supplying the order, confirm the terms with them and coordinate quality checks, aggregation and transportation.

This approach reduces the risk of farmers transporting produce without a dependable route to sale. It also gives buyers greater confidence that the quantity and quality promised can actually be delivered.

Kuapa Dwaso does not intend to purchase all produce from farmers and speculate on finding buyers later. Farmers retain ownership of their produce until they accept a sale. Our role is to coordinate the relationship, make the terms transparent and support reliable fulfilment.

## A practical path into the market

Starting an agricultural business independently can be difficult. Existing operators already possess years of market knowledge, farmer relationships, buyer connections and an understanding of the operational challenges that may not be visible from outside the sector.

The Extech team advised us to rely on experienced partners while we learn, conduct real transactions and build the capacity to operate more independently.

Our discussion led to an initial understanding that Extech Agricultural Services is willing to support Kuapa Dwaso during its early stages. The potential collaboration includes helping us connect with farmers in areas where we plan to operate, supporting the fulfilment of controlled pilot orders and creating a path to larger-volume opportunities for produce such as maize.

This is important because it gives Kuapa Dwaso a more practical starting point. Rather than trying to build an entire agricultural network from nothing, we can begin by learning alongside an organisation that already understands the sector and has relevant relationships.

The arrangement will be defined more precisely as we prepare for operations. Crops, quantities, quality requirements, pricing, payment periods, responsibilities and delivery conditions must all be agreed for each transaction.

## Selecting crops through evidence

Maize received particular attention during the discussion because it can be traded in larger quantities and may offer access to established buyers and processors. Other crops remain under consideration.

We have not selected a final crop simply because it is commonly produced or easier to store. The first crop or limited crop group must be supported by clear evidence of both demand and supply.

Our selection will consider:

- confirmed buyer demand;
- available farmer supply;
- production and harvest periods;
- quality requirements;
- shelf life and expected losses;
- collection and transportation costs;
- payment behaviour;
- and the margin remaining after fulfilment costs.

The partnership opportunity with Extech gives us another way to gather this evidence and test our assumptions through a small, controlled transaction.

## Partnerships are part of the strategy

Our visit reinforced an important principle: Kuapa Dwaso does not need to own every part of the agricultural value chain to improve how it works.

Farmers, buyers, agricultural businesses, transport providers, processors and warehouse operators already hold different parts of the knowledge and infrastructure required to move produce successfully.

Kuapa Dwaso can create value by coordinating these participants around verified demand, clear quality expectations, transparent payment terms and accountable fulfilment.

During our early stages, partnerships can help us:

- reach and verify farmers;
- understand production capacity and harvest periods;
- access credible buyers;
- coordinate transportation;
- use temporary storage when a transaction requires it;
- and learn from real operational conditions before making major investments.

Our goal is not dependence without direction. It is to learn responsibly, build a reliable transaction history and gradually develop the relationships and operating knowledge needed to stand on our own.

## What we are taking forward

The Extech visit gave us a clearer sequence for Kuapa Dwaso’s early development:

> Validate demand → identify suitable crops → verify farmers → agree on quality and payment terms → coordinate logistics → complete a controlled pilot → measure the results → repeat what works

Our immediate objective is to demonstrate that Kuapa Dwaso can reliably coordinate produce from farmers to confirmed buyers while creating value for everyone involved.

We will measure the quantities sourced and accepted, transportation and handling costs, delivery time, payment periods, losses, farmer experience, buyer experience and the margin remaining after each transaction.

These results will provide stronger evidence than projections alone. They will also help us determine where storage may eventually be required and whether future warehouse investment is commercially justified.

## Learning before scaling

The visit to Extech Agricultural Services did more than change our thinking about warehouses. It reminded us that agriculture is built on trust, experience and relationships.

The stories shared by farmers and traders in Tarkwa helped us understand the problem: farmers need dependable buyers, buyers need dependable supply, and fragmented transportation adds cost and risk for everyone.

Our discussion with Extech helped us define a practical way to begin addressing that problem—through confirmed demand, controlled pilots and partnerships with people already working within the agricultural ecosystem.

We are grateful for the opportunity to learn and for Extech Agricultural Services’ willingness to explore an early collaboration with Kuapa Dwaso. As we move towards our pilot, we will continue grounding our decisions in field evidence and real operational conditions.
