# Kuapa Dwaso Operating Model

## Detailed Business Direction and Phased Operating Plan

**Status:** Current business source of truth

**Last updated:** 10 August 2026

**Project:** Kuapa Dwaso
**Meaning:** “Farmer’s Market” in Akan

---

## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines how Kuapa Dwaso is intended to operate based on:

- conversations with farmers and traders around Tarkwa;
- the Friday farmer engagement;
- the visit to Extech Agricultural Services in Takoradi on 7 August 2026;
- the warehouse visit facilitated by Extech;
- feedback received from competition judges; and
- subsequent decisions made by the Kuapa Dwaso team.

It separates the business model we have already chosen from assumptions that still need to be tested during the pilot.

Where an older product document describes Kuapa Dwaso as beginning with an owned or fully operated warehouse, this document represents the newer direction. Warehousing remains part of the long-term vision, but it is not the starting operating model.

---

## 2. Executive Summary

Kuapa Dwaso is a demand-led agricultural market-linkage, aggregation and fulfilment service.

The business connects farmers who need dependable access to markets with buyers who need dependable access to agricultural produce. Kuapa Dwaso verifies requirements and supply, communicates transaction terms, coordinates quality control, aggregates commercially viable quantities, arranges logistics and records the transaction through to farmer settlement.

Kuapa Dwaso does **not** generally buy produce from farmers and resell it as its own stock. The farmer remains the owner until a sale is accepted under the agreed transaction terms. Ownership then passes directly from the farmer to the buyer. Kuapa Dwaso acts as the trusted coordinator between them.

The initial business will be asset-light and partnership-led:

- buyer demand is confirmed before produce is moved;
- farmers are matched to confirmed demand;
- produce is inspected against buyer-approved requirements;
- orders are aggregated to commercially viable quantities;
- transport is coordinated using logistics partners;
- existing facilities are used only when temporary storage is necessary; and
- Kuapa Dwaso earns an initial commission of 10% on completed produce sales.

Transportation and storage are not included automatically in the 10% commission. They are negotiated separately for each transaction and disclosed before the farmer accepts the sale.

Kuapa Dwaso does not plan to operate its own warehouse initially. Owned warehouse infrastructure is a longer-term ambition, currently projected at approximately three years, and will proceed only where actual demand, transaction volume, storage utilisation and financial performance justify it.

## 3. The Problem Kuapa Dwaso Solves

### 3.1 Farmers cannot always find reliable buyers

Farmers may harvest produce and begin paying for transportation before knowing whether a dependable buyer is available. After the produce reaches a city or market, the farmer may have limited bargaining power because:

- transport costs have already been incurred;
- the produce may be perishable;
- returning with the produce may be impractical;
- a trader may not have funds available immediately; and
- the farmer may feel pressured to accept a low price or delayed payment.

This can turn a normal sale into an informal credit arrangement that the farmer did not originally intend to provide.

### 3.2 Buyers cannot always find reliable sellers

Traders, processors and other buyers also face uncertainty. They need confidence that:

- the requested crop is genuinely available;
- the required quantity can be assembled;
- the produce meets their specifications;
- delivery will occur on the agreed date; and
- the supply relationship can be repeated.

Many traders purchase through other intermediaries rather than directly from farmers. This creates additional distance between demand and production.

### 3.3 Fragmented transport increases costs

Poor farm and village roads may prevent trucks from reaching production areas directly. Produce can pass through several stages:

> Farm or village → manual loading → tricycle → collection point → truck → buyer or market

Every stage introduces cost, delay, handling risk and the possibility of loss.

### 3.4 The underlying market failure

The central problem is not simply the absence of an online marketplace. It is the lack of a dependable process connecting:

> verified buyer demand → suitable farmer supply → agreed terms → quality verification → viable transport → delivery → payment

Kuapa Dwaso exists to coordinate this process.

## 4. Kuapa Dwaso’s Core Promise

### To farmers

Kuapa Dwaso helps farmers:

- reach dependable buyers;
- understand demand before moving produce;
- retain ownership until they knowingly accept a sale;
- see the price, commission, payment period and applicable charges in advance;
- avoid being forced into an unknown credit arrangement after reaching the market;
- combine their quantities with other farmers where aggregation is required;
- access coordinated transportation; and
- maintain clear evidence and transaction records.

Farmer empowerment means giving farmers better information, stronger choices and more dependable routes to market. Kuapa Dwaso does not empower farmers by taking ownership away from them.

### To buyers

Kuapa Dwaso helps buyers:

- communicate exact produce requirements;
- reach verified farmers and supply networks;
- obtain commercially viable aggregated quantities;
- receive produce checked against agreed specifications;
- coordinate collection and delivery; and
- build repeatable supply relationships.

### To the wider supply network

Kuapa Dwaso provides a structured way for farmers, buyers, transporters, agricultural partners and future storage partners to work together around one confirmed transaction.

## 5. What Kuapa Dwaso Is—and Is Not

### Kuapa Dwaso is

- a farmer-to-buyer market-linkage service;
- a demand and supply coordinator;
- an agricultural aggregation and fulfilment coordinator;
- a quality-verification and transaction-evidence layer;
- a logistics coordinator; and
- a platform for building dependable agricultural trading relationships.

### Kuapa Dwaso is not initially

- a speculative produce trader;
- the owner of farmers’ produce;
- a business that collects produce before identifying demand;
- an owner or operator of warehouses;
- a lender to farmers or buyers;
- an input-credit provider;
- a provider of uneconomically small individual deliveries;
- a USSD-dependent service; or
- a generic listing website that leaves farmers and buyers to coordinate everything themselves.

## 6. Participants and Responsibilities

### 6.1 Farmer or producer

The farmer:

- provides accurate information about available produce;
- confirms quantity, location, expected harvest date and condition;
- allows agreed quality verification;
- reviews the buyer’s price and payment terms;
- reviews Kuapa Dwaso’s commission and any separate charges;
- decides whether to accept the proposed sale; and
- supplies the quantity and quality accepted.

The farmer owns the produce until ownership passes directly to the buyer under the accepted transaction terms.

### 6.2 Buyer

Initial buyers may include traders, processors, wholesalers, institutions and other repeat bulk purchasers.

The buyer:

- provides the crop, quantity and quality requirements;
- states the destination and required delivery date;
- agrees to a price or price-determination method;
- discloses the payment period;
- makes any required deposit or financial commitment;
- accepts or rejects produce only according to the pre-agreed criteria; and
- releases payment according to the accepted terms.

### 6.3 Kuapa Dwaso

Kuapa Dwaso:

- verifies the buyer and captures the order requirements;
- identifies farmers capable of fulfilling the order;
- communicates the complete commercial terms to each farmer;
- obtains farmer acceptance before confirming supply;
- performs or coordinates quality checks before produce moves;
- records photographs, quantities, weights and other required evidence;
- aggregates supply to a commercially viable quantity;
- coordinates collection and transportation;
- manages delivery and acceptance records;
- follows up on buyer payment;
- records the commission and other agreed charges; and
- coordinates settlement to the farmers.

Kuapa Dwaso may hold physical custody temporarily during collection, aggregation, transit coordination or partner storage without becoming the owner of the produce.

### 6.4 Transport and logistics providers

Transport partners may include manual loaders, tricycle operators, truck operators and other route-specific providers.

They are responsible for:

- collecting and carrying the agreed quantity;
- protecting the produce while it is in their custody;
- following the agreed route and schedule;
- confirming loading and delivery quantities;
- reporting delays, damage or losses; and
- providing the evidence required at custody-transfer points.

### 6.5 Agricultural and storage partners

Partners may support Kuapa Dwaso with:

- farmer introductions;
- local agricultural knowledge;
- access to buyers and processors;
- temporary aggregation or storage;
- quality and commodity expertise;
- logistics relationships; and
- larger-volume purchasing opportunities.

These partnerships allow Kuapa Dwaso to learn and operate without trying to own every part of the value chain from the beginning.

## 7. End-to-End Transaction Flow

### Step 1: Capture confirmed buyer demand

Kuapa Dwaso obtains the buyer’s:

- crop and variety;
- required quantity and unit;
- quality specification;
- delivery destination;
- required date or recurring schedule;
- acceptable price or pricing method;
- payment method;
- payment period; and
- rejection conditions.

The buyer must demonstrate meaningful commitment before Kuapa Dwaso begins costly aggregation. During the pilot, Kuapa Dwaso should avoid financing buyer orders with its own funds.

### Step 2: Assess buyer reliability

Finding a buyer is not sufficient. Kuapa Dwaso evaluates:

- identity and business legitimacy;
- previous payment behaviour, where available;
- order frequency;
- ability to purchase the stated quantity;
- clarity and consistency of quality requirements;
- history of acceptance or rejection;
- willingness to provide a deposit or other commitment; and
- likelihood of becoming a repeat customer.

The early pilot will begin with a controlled group of traders. Those who order consistently, communicate clearly and pay reliably can become part of the initial customer base. Larger processors or institutional buyers may later serve as anchor customers.

### Step 3: Identify suitable farmer supply

Kuapa Dwaso checks its farmer and partner network for:

- the required crop;
- available quantity;
- expected harvest period;
- location and collection point;
- road and transport conditions;
- likely quality; and
- previous fulfilment reliability.

The business will build a verified farmer network over time rather than source randomly for every order.

### Step 4: Present the offer to farmers

Before the farmer agrees, Kuapa Dwaso communicates:

- the buyer’s price;
- the quantity requested;
- the quality specification;
- the 10% Kuapa Dwaso commission;
- separately negotiated transport or storage charges, if applicable;
- who is responsible for each charge;
- the expected collection and delivery dates;
- the buyer’s payment period; and
- the farmer’s expected net settlement.

The farmer can accept or reject the terms. Kuapa Dwaso does not move the produce until the farmer has accepted them.

### Step 5: Confirm supply with the buyer

After enough farmers accept the offer, Kuapa Dwaso confirms the available quantity and fulfilment plan with the buyer.

No promise should be made to a buyer until the supply, quality, logistics and commercial terms have been checked.

### Step 6: Inspect the produce before movement

Kuapa Dwaso performs or coordinates quality control using the buyer-approved specification.

The inspection may record:

- crop and variety;
- maturity or ripeness;
- moisture level where relevant;
- visible disease or pest damage;
- contamination;
- physical damage;
- size, grade or appearance;
- packaging condition;
- accepted and rejected quantity;
- weight and unit;
- photographs or video;
- inspection time and location; and
- the person responsible for the inspection.

Samples may be retained or shared where the crop and order justify them.

### Step 7: Aggregate commercially viable quantities

Kuapa Dwaso does not intend to fulfil very small orders that cannot support the cost of collection and transportation.

For each route or destination, the business will establish:

- a minimum individual order;
- a minimum total delivery-run quantity or value;
- an order cutoff time;
- a scheduled delivery day; and
- the conditions under which a run is confirmed, postponed or cancelled.

Multiple trader orders can be combined into one viable delivery. For example, a recurring Tarkwa delivery day may be introduced after the pilot confirms the route economics. Any example such as Friday delivery remains subject to validation.

### Step 8: Coordinate collection and transport

Kuapa Dwaso identifies the required movement stages and agrees on:

- collection points;
- vehicle types;
- loading and unloading responsibilities;
- departure and arrival times;
- transport cost;
- responsibility for payment;
- custody-transfer points; and
- procedures for delay, damage or loss.

Weights, quantities and condition are recorded whenever custody changes materially.

### Step 9: Deliver and obtain buyer acceptance

At delivery, the buyer checks the produce against the agreed specification.

The acceptance record should contain:

- delivered quantity and weight;
- accepted quantity;
- rejected quantity and documented reason;
- delivery time;
- condition on arrival;
- relevant photographs;
- buyer representative; and
- acknowledgement of receipt.

A buyer should not introduce new rejection criteria after the produce has already moved.

### Step 10: Receive buyer funds

Payment periods may differ by crop and buyer. Some maize transactions may settle immediately, while another buyer or crop may have an agreed delay such as one week.

Kuapa Dwaso communicates this period before the farmer accepts the sale. If the farmer does not accept the payment period, Kuapa Dwaso does not commit that farmer’s produce.

The farmer is paid when the buyer releases the funds. Kuapa Dwaso will not initially promise to advance payment from its own working capital.

### Step 11: Settle the farmer and close the transaction

Kuapa Dwaso records:

- gross produce sale value;
- the 10% commission;
- separately agreed charges assigned to the farmer;
- separately agreed charges assigned to the buyer;
- total funds received;
- net amount due to each farmer;
- farmer payment date; and
- transaction completion status.

The general farmer settlement calculation is:

> Gross produce sale value
>
> − Kuapa Dwaso commission
>
> − only those separate charges the farmer accepted
>
> = Net farmer settlement

Transport and storage are not automatically deducted from the farmer. Their payer is agreed and disclosed for each transaction.

## 8. Ownership, Custody and Risk

Ownership and custody are different.

- The farmer owns the produce before the sale.
- Kuapa Dwaso may coordinate or temporarily hold custody without buying it.
- A transport provider may hold custody while the farmer still owns the produce.
- Ownership passes directly from the farmer to the buyer at the point defined in the accepted transaction terms.
- Kuapa Dwaso does not become the owner between those two parties.

Every transaction should identify responsibility during:

- collection from the farmer;
- movement to an aggregation point;
- temporary holding;
- loading;
- transportation;
- unloading; and
- buyer inspection and acceptance.

If a loss occurs, the evidence should establish when the condition changed and who held custody at that point.

---

## 9. Quality Control and Rejection Management

Kuapa Dwaso intends to inspect produce before taking it to the buyer. This protects the farmer, buyer and platform, but it does not remove the need for agreed acceptance criteria.

The quality-control process must therefore be:

- crop-specific rather than generic;
- based on the buyer’s requirements;
- completed before transport;
- supported by measurements and visual evidence;
- repeatable across farmers supplying the same order; and
- connected to a documented delivery-acceptance process.

If produce fails inspection before collection, it should not be included in that order.

If the buyer rejects produce that matched the recorded pre-dispatch specification, Kuapa Dwaso will use the inspection, custody and delivery evidence to determine whether:

- deterioration occurred during transport;
- the buyer applied an unagreed standard;
- the farmer’s original information was inaccurate; or
- another party caused the loss.

Rejected produce must not be left without an owner, destination or recorded resolution.

## 10. Commercial Model

### 10.1 Sales commission

Kuapa Dwaso’s current revenue assumption is a 10% commission on the gross produce value of every sale completed through the service.

The commission:

- applies only when a sale is completed;
- is shown to the farmer before acceptance;
- is recorded separately from the value belonging to the farmer; and
- will be adjusted if pilot evidence shows that another percentage produces a fairer and more sustainable result.

The 10% figure is the current operating hypothesis, not a claim that the final percentage has already been validated across all crops and transaction sizes.

### 10.2 Transportation

Transportation is negotiated separately because its cost depends on:

- crop and packaging;
- quantity and vehicle;
- farm access;
- number of movement stages;
- route and destination;
- fuel and loading costs; and
- whether several orders share one delivery run.

The buyer, farmer or both may bear the cost according to the transaction agreement. The responsibility must be disclosed before acceptance.

### 10.3 Storage

Storage is not included automatically in the commission and is not part of every transaction.

During the early phase, temporary storage will be arranged only where required and will rely on an assessed partner facility. The storage price, duration, responsibility and payment method will be agreed separately.

### 10.4 No initial buyer financing

Kuapa Dwaso will not initially finance buyers or purchase farmers’ produce with its own money. Deferred buyer terms are permitted only when:

- the buyer is considered sufficiently reliable;
- the payment period is explicit;
- the farmer knowingly accepts the delay; and
- the transaction does not expose Kuapa Dwaso to an unapproved working-capital obligation.

## 11. Crop and Corridor Selection

Kuapa Dwaso has crop candidates but has not made a final selection.

Candidates discussed so far include:

- maize;
- pepper;
- cabbage;
- rice; and
- other produce supported by verified demand and supply.

The initial crop or tightly limited crop set will be selected only after comparing:

- confirmed buyer demand;
- buyer reliability and purchasing frequency;
- farmer supply and harvest periods;
- reachable production communities;
- quality-assessment complexity;
- shelf life and expected loss;
- collection and transport requirements;
- destination-market demand;
- price volatility;
- achievable contribution margin; and
- the ability to repeat the transaction.

The first operating corridor must connect specific production communities to specific buyers or destination markets. Kuapa Dwaso will not claim to serve every crop or every part of Ghana at launch.

## 12. Extech Agricultural Services Partnership

On Friday, 7 August 2026, Ms Catherine Bruce Vochere and Mr Kevin Amisom Nchorbuno visited Extech Agricultural Services in Takoradi. Extech also facilitated a warehouse visit and shared practical guidance about entering the agricultural sector.

The central lesson was that a new agricultural business should not attempt to build every relationship and capability independently. It should learn through established operators until it has enough experience, trust and transaction history to stand more independently.

Extech expressed willingness to support Kuapa Dwaso’s early development by:

- helping connect Kuapa Dwaso with farmers around intended operating areas;
- supporting the sourcing required for small, controlled pilot orders; and
- helping create larger-volume opportunities for produce such as maize.

The exact commercial role must be documented before a transaction. Depending on the opportunity, Extech may act as a farmer-network partner, sourcing partner, buyer, buyer-linkage partner or a combination of these roles.

The relationship does not remove Kuapa Dwaso’s responsibility to document:

- crop and quantity;
- quality requirements;
- price and commission;
- payment period;
- farmer ownership;
- transport responsibility;
- delivery and rejection conditions; and
- each party’s role in the transaction.

## 13. Farmer Access and Communication

The Friday farmer engagement challenged the assumption that USSD must be the primary way farmers access Kuapa Dwaso.

The farmers engaged indicated that many either had a smartphone or had someone in their household who could help them use one. Based on this evidence, the initial access model is:

- a lightweight smartphone experience;
- farmer self-registration where practical;
- assisted registration by a trusted household member or Kuapa Dwaso representative; and
- one-way SMS notifications for important transaction updates.

USSD and two-way SMS are not required for the initial pilot. This decision may be revisited if future field evidence from other communities demonstrates a meaningful accessibility gap.

Technology access must not be confused with farmer consent. A household member may assist with the interface, but the farmer must understand and accept the commercial terms governing their produce.

## 14. Pilot Model

The pilot should involve a controlled number of farmers, traders and transactions while still using a commercially sensible total quantity.

“Small pilot” means limited operational exposure, not fulfilling tiny orders that cannot support transport costs.

The pilot will test:

> Buyer demand → farmer sourcing → farmer acceptance → quality control → aggregation → transport → delivery → buyer acceptance → buyer payment → farmer settlement

### The pilot must record

- number of participating farmers;
- number and type of buyers;
- requested, sourced, delivered, accepted and rejected quantities;
- farmer price and buyer price;
- Kuapa Dwaso commission;
- transport, loading, handling and temporary-storage costs;
- collection and delivery times;
- weight and condition changes;
- buyer payment period;
- farmer settlement period;
- complaints and disputes;
- contribution margin; and
- whether farmers and buyers want to transact again.

### Pilot success must demonstrate

- credible demand existed before collection;
- enough supply could be assembled;
- the quality requirements were understood and met;
- the delivery was operationally manageable;
- farmers were paid according to the terms they accepted;
- every material cost was recorded;
- the transaction produced an understandable financial result; and
- there is credible interest in repeating the transaction.

Exact success thresholds will be set after the crop, corridor, buyers and pilot quantity are confirmed.

## 15. Phased Warehouse Strategy

### Phase 1: No Kuapa Dwaso-operated warehouse

Kuapa Dwaso will begin without constructing or operating a warehouse.

The business will prioritise:

- confirming demand;
- building farmer and buyer relationships;
- coordinating commercially viable transactions;
- understanding logistics;
- learning crop-specific quality requirements; and
- measuring real unit economics.

Existing partner facilities or temporary aggregation points may be used only where a transaction requires them.

### Phase 2: Partner storage where justified

As transaction volume grows, Kuapa Dwaso may establish formal arrangements with existing warehouses or agricultural businesses in corridors where storage provides a clear benefit.

Partner storage must define:

- custody and liability;
- accepted crops and conditions;
- intake and inspection procedures;
- storage duration;
- fees and payment timing;
- security and inventory checking;
- spoilage and shrinkage responsibility;
- insurance arrangements; and
- withdrawal and dispatch procedures.

### Phase 3: Potential owned warehouse

Kuapa Dwaso currently projects that owned warehouse infrastructure may become appropriate in approximately three years. This is a strategic ambition, not an unconditional construction date.

The business will build or finance a warehouse only after establishing:

- a proven market in the area;
- consistent farmer supply;
- repeat buyer demand;
- sufficient storage demand;
- expected utilisation capable of supporting operating and debt costs;
- positive transaction economics;
- a suitable location and crop mix;
- adequate working-capital and risk reserves; and
- evidence that ownership is more valuable than continued partnership.

Bank financing may be considered after the business has an operating record that can support the loan case.

## 16. Future Owned-Warehouse Operating Principles

The following principles apply only when Kuapa Dwaso begins operating its own warehouse or assumes equivalent custody responsibility.

### 16.1 Farmer ownership

Storing produce does not transfer ownership to Kuapa Dwaso. The farmer continues to own it until an accepted sale transfers ownership directly to a buyer.

### 16.2 Storage payment

- For produce intended for near-immediate sale, agreed storage charges may be deducted from sale proceeds.
- For produce intentionally stored for a longer period, the farmer pays the storage charge upfront, even if the farmer later sells through Kuapa Dwaso.
- Every price and deduction is disclosed before intake.

### 16.3 Insurance-backed custody protection

Protection for defined losses caused by Kuapa Dwaso’s own custody or operational failures will be included in warehouse pricing rather than presented as an optional add-on.

Kuapa Dwaso does not intend to pay valid claims from ordinary operating cash. A qualified insurer will back the agreed coverage, while Kuapa Dwaso manages evidence collection and the farmer-facing claims process.

Coverage will not automatically include every form of deterioration or loss. The policy and farmer agreement must distinguish:

- Kuapa Dwaso-attributable custody failures;
- pre-existing damage or disease;
- ordinary deterioration within an agreed tolerance;
- farmer-requested storage beyond a recommended safe period;
- transporter-caused loss; and
- any separately named insured events.

### 16.4 Crop-specific intake inspection

Warehouse intake will not rely on a generic checklist. The agent will complete fields appropriate to the crop, including maturity, damage, infestation, moisture, contamination, condition and accepted or rejected quantity. Photographs and other evidence will support the record.

### 16.5 Inventory inspections and independent verification

Warehouse agents will perform scheduled inventory-status checks, including a weekly check at the assigned time.

The warehouse manager or another authorised supervisor will conduct random rechecks, scale verification and periodic full stock counts. The person responsible for day-to-day custody should not be the only person validating every inventory result.

### 16.6 Approved preservation treatments

Where a crop such as properly dried maize can safely support longer-term preservation, Kuapa Dwaso may offer an approved treatment service.

No treatment will be used unless it is:

- lawful and approved for the crop and intended use;
- applied by a competent person;
- recorded by product, dosage, date and responsible person;
- disclosed to and accepted by the farmer;
- compatible with buyer requirements; and
- supported by an appropriate withholding or safe-use period where required.

## 17. Financial Discipline

Kuapa Dwaso must keep the following values separate:

- total produce value processed;
- value belonging to farmers;
- Kuapa Dwaso commission revenue;
- transport and storage amounts collected on behalf of others;
- buyer receivables;
- farmer settlements due and paid;
- refunds, rejections and claims;
- direct fulfilment costs;
- operating expenses; and
- actual contribution margin.

The business should not describe gross produce value as Kuapa Dwaso revenue.

For every crop and corridor, the team needs to understand:

- minimum viable order quantity;
- cost per unit collected and delivered;
- expected loss and rejection rate;
- collection-to-payment cycle;
- commission earned;
- contribution margin; and
- cash required before buyer payment.

The admin and financial reporting process should make it possible to determine whether transaction growth is improving or weakening the business.

- gross produce value processed;
- commission revenue;
- contribution margin per transaction;
- logistics and handling cost;
- cash collected;
- farmer settlements due and paid; and
- repeatable margin by crop and corridor.

1. Kuapa Dwaso connects farmers and buyers; it does not generally purchase produce itself.
2. Farmers retain ownership until an accepted sale transfers it directly to the buyer.
3. Demand is confirmed before produce is aggregated or transported.
4. Farmers see and accept price, commission, payment period and applicable charges before committing.
5. Kuapa Dwaso performs or coordinates quality control before delivery.
6. The initial commission is 10% of completed produce sales.
7. Transportation and storage are negotiated separately.
8. The initial model does not depend on a Kuapa Dwaso-operated warehouse.
9. Partnerships are the preferred entry strategy.
10. Extech Agricultural Services is willing to explore early support involving farmer access, pilots and larger-volume opportunities.
11. The pilot will begin with a controlled buyer group and commercially viable aggregated orders.
12. No final crop will be selected until demand and supply evidence support it.

## 21. Overall Direction

Kuapa Dwaso’s operating sequence is:

> Understand demand
>
> → verify the buyer
>
> → identify capable farmers
>
> → agree on quality, price and payment terms
>
> → obtain farmer consent
>
> → aggregate a commercially viable quantity
>
> → coordinate transport
>
> → deliver and document acceptance
>
> → receive buyer funds
>
> → settle farmers and record the result
>
> → repeat what works

The immediate goal is to prove that Kuapa Dwaso can repeatedly coordinate dependable sales between farmers and buyers while protecting farmer choice and producing sustainable transaction economics.

The long-term goal is to become a trusted Farmer’s Market: a network through which farmers gain stronger access to markets, buyers gain dependable supply, and storage and other infrastructure are introduced only where actual operations prove they are needed.
